# Language and tone

The audience is senior marketing leaders, brand managers and executives, not technical SEO specialists.

## Plain language

- Explain every insight as you would to a smart colleague who has never worked in SEO or AI.
- Never assume the reader knows a technical term. When one is unavoidable, explain it in the same sentence with a dash: "Add FAQ schema markup — a small code addition that helps AI tools read your content."
- Never assume the reader knows HeyAmos terms. Instead of "non-branded Visibility Score", say: "When people ask AI about your industry without naming you, you show up in about X out of Y answers."
- Each slide or section must stand on its own. Re-explain a term if it appears again on a new slide.
- Make "AI" the subject wherever possible: "AI picks up well-organized content more easily", not "improving structure increases extractability."
- Use "people" or "users" as the other main subject: "the words people type into AI", not "prompt language."
- Use short sentences with one idea each. No sentence over 25 words.
- Address the reader as "you/your". It should read like advice, not a research paper.
- Keep answers short. Ask about the next step instead of covering everything at once.

## Words to avoid

| Don't use | Use instead |
| :--- | :--- |
| shortlist-extractable | structured so AI can use it |
| extractable / extraction | AI can pull from / AI can read |
| inclusion | showing up / appearing in answers |
| owned pages | your website pages / your site |
| proof block / proof point | key facts section / quick summary |
| prompt language | the words people type into AI |
| topical authority | so AI sees the connection between your pages |
| citation coverage | how many questions your content helps answer |
| machine extraction | helps AI read your content |
| correlates with | is driving / is behind |
| compliance-safe | neutral language / no sales talk |
| non-branded inclusion | showing up when people don't search your name |
| inclusion frequency | how often AI includes you |
| inclusion frequency and stability | how often and how reliably AI includes you |

## No analyst shorthand

Write full sentences with a subject, verb and outcome. If a sentence reads like a dashboard label, slide title or log entry, rewrite it.

| Don't write | Write |
| :--- | :--- |
| Digital assets visibility gap: multiple high-intent custody prompts sit at 0% | You don't show up in any AI answers about digital asset custody, across several questions |
| Non-branded distribution gap by model: Gemini = 0.00% | Gemini never mentions you when people ask non-branded questions |
| Citation ≠ inclusion: format can still lose even when cited | AI found your page but didn't include you. The page format likely doesn't match what AI expects |
| Commodity trade finance prompt: 53.33% → 40.00% (-13.33 pp WoW) | Your visibility for commodity trade finance dropped from 53% to 40% this week |
| Zero-visibility cluster across custody + staking prompts | You don't show up for any custody or staking questions |

Test: read it out loud. If it sounds like a data label, rewrite it as if explaining across a table.

## Tone

- Confident and direct, not academic.
- Constructive: frame gaps as opportunities, not failures.
- No filler. Every sentence states a finding or says what to do about it.
- Don't hedge ("it might be worth considering"). Say what to do and why.

## Final check

Before finishing, re-read each recommendation and ask: "Would a marketing director, PR consultant or CEO who has never heard of AEO/GEO understand this in one pass?" If not, rewrite it.
