# Common questions

Use this context to give a brief, direct answer in conversation. Don't paste it all; pick what answers the question.

## Why is AI picking this source?

AI doesn't rank pages the way Google does. For a decision question ("best X for Y"), the model pulls from sources it trusts and can read easily: editorial media, comparison and review sites, Wikipedia and institutional listings, and well-structured brand pages.

Three things decide whether a source gets used:

- **Trust.** AI leans on independent sources (news, reviews, directories) over brand claims. That's why earned media often outweighs the brand's own site.
- **Readability.** AI picks up well-organized content more easily: clear headings, direct answers, facts stated plainly. A beautiful page that buries the answer loses to a plain page that states it.
- **Relevance to the exact question.** AI matches sources to the decision being made. The source cited for pricing questions is often different from the one cited for "best in [city]".

HeyAmos records the actual citations behind every AI answer. It shows which pages AI looked at, what it quoted, and which pages drive positive or negative mentions. A negative theme can be traced to the page AI pulled it from — often one review site or an old article.

## What can we do? Can we influence it?

- **Your own content.** If AI has nothing to cite on a topic, you're invisible on it. Dedicated, well-structured pages for topics where you appear in zero answers give AI something to use. This is usually the fastest lever.
- **Earned media.** Placements in the outlets AI already cites for your category. HeyAmos ranks outlets by how often AI cites them for your prompts (`earned_media_top_sources_to_pitch`), so PR spend targets outlets that move AI answers. The HeyAmos platform also lets you buy placements in the outlets AI reads for your industry.

## Search volume / demand / prompt volume

AI searches happen in conversation, so nearly every prompt is a unique long-tail query with a volume of one. To measure real demand, HeyAmos goes back to topics and keywords: how often a topic or keyword appears inside AI conversations. That gives a reliable signal however people phrase the question.

The data comes from opted-in consumer panels, anonymized and aggregated. Like election polling, a large enough sample lets you estimate total volume. A question can be phrased 100 ways, but AI still looks for the same underlying keywords, so keyword-level volume shows what people actually ask about.

## What is a good benchmark?

It depends on the starting point:

- At 0%, the first milestone is 10%: get on the board for the prompts that matter.
- Around 20%, aim for 50%.
- At 80%, more coverage isn't the priority. Work on *how* AI talks about you: sentiment, positioning, and whether the story matches what you want to be known for.

Benchmark against competitors on the same prompts rather than an abstract number. Expect a few points of natural week-to-week movement, and give new content 3–4 weeks to get cited before judging it.

## KPIs, ROI and timeline

**Headline KPI: the non-branded Visibility Score.** When people ask AI about your category without naming you, how many answers out of 100 mention you. This reflects real discovery and new business. Branded questions inflate the blended score, because you'd expect to appear when someone names you.

Supporting KPIs, tracked period over period:

- **Citations:** how many AI answers cite your pages, compared with competitors.
- **Pages cited:** how much of your content AI actually uses.
- **Sentiment:** the positive / neutral / negative split and what drives each.
- **Earned media impact:** which press coverage AI cites, and in which answers.
- **Visibility per topic and per competitor:** where you win and where you're invisible.

**Timeline.** The baseline audit gives the starting picture. Prompts re-run on a fixed schedule, and monthly reports show what changed and why. That lets you tie actions to outcomes: content published → AI picks it up → visibility on that topic moves. First movement usually shows within weeks of AI picking up new content or coverage. Meaningful shifts build over one to three months of steady publishing and placements. Anyone promising overnight jumps is guessing.

**ROI.** Connect visibility to downstream demand. Ask leads and customers how they found you, through forms, surveys or directly.

## Data methodology

1. **Prompt set.** A fixed set of decision-stage prompts (25, 50 or 100), built from intent clusters and tailored to the category, competitors and markets.
2. **Simulations.** Each prompt runs several times per period (5 runs as standard) on each covered AI tool. AI answers vary, so one run is an anecdote and repeated runs are a measurement.
3. **Parsing.** Every response is analyzed for whether the brand is mentioned, its position, the sentiment, which competitors appear, and every source URL cited.
4. **Scoring.** Visibility is the share of answers that mention the brand. The headline score uses non-branded prompts only. Everything can be filtered by AI tool, category, topic and period.
5. **Cadence.** Prompts run on a fixed schedule (weekly for retainers), so trends are real trend lines, not two snapshots.

Two principles: **nothing is made up or approximated** — every number traces to a recorded AI response and its citations, with the verbatim extracts available. And **citations are verified, not assumed** — when HeyAmos says a page drives a negative theme, it has read what AI quoted from it.
